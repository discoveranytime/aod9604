AOD9604
the fat-loss fragment

A modified slice of human growth hormone — engineered to keep hGH’s fat-burning trick while ditching its growth effects. It went through real clinical trials for obesity, didn’t make the cut, and was never approved. Today it lives on as a WADA-banned research chemical with a loud forum fanbase. Here is the fragment, separated from the hype.

Quick read

AOD9604 is a modified version of growth hormone’s 176–191 fragment (with an added tyrosine for stability) — the tail end of hGH that researchers linked to fat breakdown without the growth effects like raised blood sugar or IGF-1. An Australian company took it through phase 2 obesity trials; it didn’t progress to approval. Later research pivoted to cartilage repair. It was never approved as a medicine, is banned in sport, and is sold online only as an unregulated research chemical.

01The fragment

What AOD9604 actually is

AOD9604 is a lab-modified fragment of human growth hormone — the 176–191 tail, stabilized with an extra tyrosine — designed to trigger fat breakdown without hGH’s growth effects. It’s the rare gray-market peptide with a real clinical-trial past.

The whole short chain is the product: growth hormone’s tail, tweaked for stability — fat signal in, growth signal out.

A slice of growth hormone

Full hGH does many things — grows tissue, raises blood sugar, lifts IGF-1. Researchers found its tail-end fragment seemed to trigger fat breakdown on its own.

Modified for the job

The natural 176–191 fragment was tweaked — a tyrosine added for stability — creating AOD9604: a purpose-built fragment, not a natural hormone.

Not hGH, not a shortcut to it

AOD9604 doesn’t meaningfully raise IGF-1 or mimic full growth hormone. Whatever it does, it does without hGH’s growth machinery — which was the entire design goal.

Plain English

Imagine growth hormone as a Swiss Army knife. Scientists snapped off the fat-burning blade, sharpened it, and threw away the rest of the knife. AOD9604 is that blade — useful-looking, but it never passed inspection as a real tool.

02The trial story

A real drug program that didn’t make it

Unlike most research chemicals, AOD9604 has a documented clinical history: development, phase 2 obesity trials, a cartilage pivot — and no approval at the end of the road.

A genuine drug-development arc — that ended without a drug.
The fragment clue

Early work suggested hGH’s C-terminal fragment could stimulate fat breakdown without the hormone’s other effects — a surgical strike instead of a systemic blast.

A real company, real trials

An Australian firm developed AOD9604 and ran phase 1 and phase 2 trials for obesity. This isn’t forum lore — it’s a documented clinical program that didn’t advance.

The pivot to cartilage

Later research explored AOD9604 for cartilage repair and osteoarthritis — a second act that also never reached approval. Two indications, zero approvals.

Plain English

Most gray-market peptides never got near a clinic. AOD9604 did — real company, real trials, real volunteers — and the program still stalled. That history is both its best credential and its honest verdict: tested, not approved.

03How it’s supposed to work

The fat signal, minus the growth

AOD9604’s pitch is selectivity: fat breakdown without growth-hormone baggage. The mechanism is the most defensible part of the story — the human results are where it thins out.

The proposed profile — fat signal without the growth baggage; human confirmation thin.
Lipolysis, selectively

The core claim: the fragment stimulates fat breakdown (lipolysis) and discourages new fat formation — hGH’s fat trick, isolated.

What it deliberately doesn’t do

By design it doesn’t raise IGF-1 or blood sugar the way full hGH does. That selectivity was the selling point — and the reason it isn’t just “baby growth hormone.”

The cartilage chapter

Later work suggested effects on cartilage cells — a plausible second mechanism that also never matured into an approved therapy.

Plain English

The design brief was surgical: keep the fat-burning, lose everything else. In the lab the fragment behaves that way. In people, the obesity trials didn’t produce a drug — which is the market’s way of saying the effect wasn’t convincing enough.

06Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

A discontinued drug program doesn’t equal a safe gray-market product. The risks of the vial are a separate file from the risks of the trials — here’s both, in boxes.

Observed

Banned in sport

Observed fact: WADA prohibits AOD9604 (S2: peptide hormones and related substances). Tested athletes have been caught and sanctioned for growth-hormone fragments.

Observed

Unregulated vials

Observed fact: what’s sold as AOD9604 has no quality oversight. Gray-market peptide testing repeatedly finds impurities, wrong doses, or substituted compounds.

Observed

The program was discontinued

Observed fact: the obesity development program was not advanced after phase 2. Whatever the full reasons, the developer walked away.

Theoretical

Fragment ≠ inert

Theoretical: “just a fragment” doesn’t mean biologically quiet — fragments can have off-target effects the parent hormone doesn’t. Never systematically studied here.

Unknown

Long-term effects

Unknown: no chronic-use safety data in humans. What prolonged fragment exposure does is uncharted.

Unknown

Interactions

Unknown: no drug-interaction studies, no data in diabetes, pregnancy, or metabolic disease — the exact populations drawn to a fat-loss peptide.

Plain English

Known: banned in sport, unregulated vials, and a discontinued program. Theoretical: fragments can surprise you. Unknown: chronic safety and interactions. The trial history answers “does it work well enough” — it doesn’t answer “is the vial safe.”
